# Sending rules: read this before you email anyone

This is a summary to get you started, checked on 2026-11-09. It is not legal advice, and laws change. Links are in [SOURCES.md](../SOURCES.md). If you are going to send at any real volume, talk to a lawyer in the country you are sending to.

## United States: commercial email

The US Federal Trade Commission's CAN-SPAM guide says the law makes no exception for business to business email. The main requirements in that guide are:

1. No false or misleading header information. The From, To and Reply To details must be accurate.
2. No deceptive subject lines.
3. The message must be clearly identified as an advertisement. You have some flexibility in how.
4. Include your valid physical postal address.
5. Clearly explain how to stop future marketing emails.
6. Honor opt outs within 10 business days, and keep the opt out working for at least 30 days after you send.

## Other countries can be stricter

- **Canada:** the government's page on its anti spam law says businesses must get consent before sending commercial electronic messages. That is a very different starting point from the US.
- **UK and EU:** these have their own privacy and electronic marketing rules, and the treatment of a person at a company can differ from a company address. We could not open the UK regulator's page when this kit was checked, so we state no detail here. Read the current guidance from the Information Commissioner's Office or your country's regulator.
- **Anywhere else:** assume there are rules, and look them up.

The rules follow the person receiving the email, so the country you are sending to matters as well as the country you are in.

## LinkedIn

LinkedIn's User Agreement bars bots and unauthorized automation to add contacts, send messages or engage. Its help pages also bar third party software that scrapes or automates activity. Treat LinkedIn as a place to read and to write by hand. Where a niche card mentions LinkedIn, it means manual reading only.

## Public lists are not free for any use

The niche cards name public sources like licence lookups and member directories. We named those from general knowledge, and did not check the terms of every one.

- Read each site's terms before you collect from it. Some forbid commercial use or bulk copying. Bar directories and financial regulator lookups are examples of where to be especially careful.
- Do not scrape a site that forbids it.
- Only keep the fields you need, and delete people who ask you to.

## Sending safely

- Use a real name, a real business and a real postal address.
- Do not send from your main business domain while you are testing.
- Set up the standard email authentication records (SPF, DKIM and DMARC) on the domain you use.
- Start small, and check every message before it goes out while you are learning.
- Stop contacting anyone who says stop, on every channel, and record it.

## The opening messages in this kit

Each one has parts in square brackets. They only work if you replace them with something true you found yourself. Never send a made up detail. The messages also leave out the postal address and opt out line, which you must add wherever the law requires them. They make no claims about past results because you have none yet in that niche. If you have real ones, add them with proof.
