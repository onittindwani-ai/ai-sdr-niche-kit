# How to set up a booked call offer

This guide helps you turn a niche card's offer into a deal an owner can say yes to. It gives no prices, because we have no real data on what you should charge. You will find your own number in the [10 conversation test](TEN-CONVERSATION-TEST.md).

## 1. Define a booked call in writing

Most arguments in this business are about what counts as a call. Agree on it before you start.

A booked call counts when all of these are true:

- The person matches the customer description you both signed off.
- The person agreed to the call knowing who it is with and what it is about.
- It is on the owner's calendar, with a real date and time.
- The person turned up, or you both agreed in advance how no shows are handled.

Write down what does not count: a wrong company size, a job seeker, a salesperson pitching the owner, or someone who says they were misled.

## 2. Agree who approves what

- The owner approves the customer list before anyone is contacted.
- The owner approves the wording before anything is sent.
- You agree on which claims are allowed, and each one has proof.
- A stop request is obeyed every time, on every channel, right away.

## 3. Choose a pricing shape

There are 3 common shapes. Choose by what the owner can accept and what the rules allow.

| Shape | Good when | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat monthly fee | You want steady income, or the niche is regulated | The owner may feel they are paying with no guarantee |
| Pay per qualified booked call | The owner distrusts retainers and the niche has no referral rules | Disputes about what qualifies, and it is not allowed everywhere |
| Small setup fee plus pay per call | You have list building and setup costs to cover | Needs the clearest definition of all |

**Regulated niches: use a flat fee and get it reviewed.** Paying per patient, client or referral can be illegal or restricted in health, legal and financial work. Each regulated niche card has a compliance note. Read it before you pick a shape.

## 4. How to work out your price without made up numbers

Ask the owner these during your conversations. Use their answers, not an average from the internet:

1. What is one new customer worth to you, over the time they usually stay?
2. How many booked calls does it usually take you to win one?
3. What have you paid for leads before, and what happened?

Your price has to leave the owner clearly ahead on their own numbers. If it does not, the niche or the offer is wrong.

## 5. Run a pilot before a contract

- Keep it short, with a fixed end date.
- Agree in advance what a good result looks like.
- Agree what happens if it does not work.
- Never promise a number of calls unless you have delivered that number before in that niche.

## 6. Say what you are

Tell the owner what is automated and what you check yourself. Do not pretend an AI wrote nothing, and do not let a message claim a human did something a human did not do. Use a real sender name and a real business address.

## What you must never promise

Revenue, a specific number of customers, a position in a market, a compliance outcome, or a result you have not produced before. If you do have real results, show them with proof and say how they were measured.
