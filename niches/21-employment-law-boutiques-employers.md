# Employment law boutiques serving small employers

**Default rank 41 of 50. Total 69 out of 100.** Legal. Regulated: legal. Written for the US, needs local rules and sources elsewhere.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Partner at a small employment law practice that defends and advises employers.

## Who the booked calls are with

Owners and HR managers at firms with enough staff to need an outside employment lawyer.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

Retainers from growing employers are steady, but owners tend to look for a lawyer only when something has gone wrong. A partner wants that relationship started earlier.

## A ready offer

I book calls with owners and HR managers who want an employment lawyer on hand before they have a problem. You approve all messaging and pay a flat service fee.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], your [real article or talk] on handbook updates was useful. Employers often look for counsel only after a problem starts, and I can arrange earlier introductions with owners and HR managers near you, with your approval on every message. Is that worth a short talk?

## Where to find the leads

- Firm websites and local bar employment section speaker lists
- Google Maps for employment attorneys for employers
- For their customers: local chambers of commerce and companies posting HR roles

## What to avoid

- Mentioning a lawsuit or complaint against the prospect
- Bulk collecting bar directories, some of which forbid commercial use
- Anything that reads as legal advice

## Compliance note

Lawyer advertising and direct contact are governed by state bar rules based on the ABA Model Rules (Rules 7.2 and 7.3 cover advertising and direct contact). Payment per booked client can raise problems around recommendations and fee sharing. Use a flat fee and get the firm's written sign off on the message and the list. This is not legal advice.
