# Commercial HVAC service contractors selling maintenance contracts

**Default rank 21 of 50. Total 75 out of 100.** Commercial trades. Not in a regulated group. The idea works in most countries, though the named lead sources are often US ones.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Owner of a commercial HVAC shop with a small service team who wants more contract work and fewer emergency only customers.

## Who the booked calls are with

Facility managers at schools, churches, small manufacturers, office parks and medical buildings.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

Maintenance contracts smooth out the slow months, but the people who could sign them are not searching until something breaks. The owner has dispatch to run and no time to open those doors.

## A ready offer

I book calls with facility managers who are a fit for a planned maintenance agreement. You get the name, the building type and what they said, before you get on the call.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], your [real service line from their site] work caught my eye. Contract customers are usually won before the rooftop unit fails, and that is the conversation I set up. I find facility managers near you and book the ones open to a maintenance quote. Should I send you a sample of 10 buildings?

## Where to find the leads

- State mechanical or HVAC contractor licence lists
- ACCA and local mechanical contractor association member pages where public
- For their customers: school district and church staff pages, and local industrial park tenant lists

## What to avoid

- Shops with a full time sales rep already working contracts
- Residential comfort companies, whose buyers are homeowners
- Quoting energy savings you have not measured
