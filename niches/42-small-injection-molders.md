# Small plastic injection molders

**Default rank 7 of 50. Total 80 out of 100.** Manufacturing and industrial. Not in a regulated group. The idea works in most countries, though the named lead sources are often US ones.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Owner or general manager of a small molding company with a handful of presses.

## Who the booked calls are with

Product developers, sourcing managers and engineers at companies bringing parts back from overseas.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

A single program can run for years. Small molders do not exhibit widely and depend on repeat customers who eventually move on or close.

## A ready offer

I book calls with sourcing managers and product teams looking for a domestic molder, starting with parts that match your press sizes.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], with your [real press range or material], you likely suit programs too small for the big molders. I find sourcing managers and product teams looking for that kind of partner and book the intro. What part sizes would you most like to see more of?

## Where to find the leads

- Thomasnet and Plastics Industry Association member lists where public
- State manufacturing directories
- For their customers: product launch news, engineering job posts and trade show exhibitor lists

## What to avoid

- Calling a program won when only a quote was asked for
- Promising tooling lead times
- Medical or food parts unless the molder holds the right certifications
