# Home care agencies seeking referral partners

**Default rank 50 of 50. Total 58 out of 100.** Health. Regulated: health. Written for the US, needs local rules and sources elsewhere.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Owner of a small non medical or skilled home care agency, usually running operations and staffing at once.

## Who the booked calls are with

Hospital discharge planners, social workers, elder law attorneys and senior living staff.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

Referral partners bring steady clients, and the owner spends most days filling caregiver shifts. Building those relationships takes time they do not have.

## A ready offer

I arrange informational intro calls with discharge planners and elder law attorneys for a flat service fee that has nothing to do with how many clients you get.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], it looks like you are hands on with [real service or area]. I can arrange introductions with discharge planners and elder law attorneys who want to know the local options, for a flat fee that never depends on how many clients come from it. Would that be okay with your compliance adviser?

## Where to find the leads

- State home care licence lists where they exist
- Google Maps for home care agencies
- For their customers: hospital case management departments and elder law firm sites

## What to avoid

- Any fee per referral, per patient or per client
- Giving gifts or meals to referral sources without review
- Contacting patients or their families directly

## Compliance note

High risk. Paying or receiving anything in return for referring patients whose care a federal health program may pay can be a crime under the federal Anti Kickback Statute (42 U.S.C. 1320a-7b). A pay per booked call model is the wrong offer here. Use a flat fee only, and get a health care lawyer to review the arrangement first. This is not legal advice.
