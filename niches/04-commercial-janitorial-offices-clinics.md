# Commercial janitorial companies for offices and clinics

**Default rank 28 of 50. Total 73 out of 100.** Commercial trades. Not in a regulated group. The idea works in most countries, though the named lead sources are often US ones.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Owner of a local janitorial company with night crews, often still doing walkthroughs and quotes personally.

## Who the booked calls are with

Office managers, practice managers and building managers at small offices and outpatient clinics.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

Customers leave quietly and a replacement has to be found, so a steady flow of walkthroughs matters more than a one time burst. The owner runs crews at night and sells in whatever daylight is left.

## A ready offer

I book walkthroughs with office and practice managers inside your route area, so new accounts land near the crews you already pay to drive there.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], your [real detail, such as a clinic or office they clean] tells me you already do the harder buildings. I can book walkthroughs with office and practice managers close to your current routes, so new accounts do not stretch your crews. Would a list of nearby buildings be useful to look at?

## Where to find the leads

- Google Maps for commercial cleaning by city
- ISSA and BSCAI member search where public
- For their customers: office park tenant lists and clinic websites that name a practice manager

## What to avoid

- Franchise units whose head office controls marketing
- Anyone who will not state their insurance and bonding in writing
- Competing only on price, which is where this niche gets crowded
