# Immigration law firms serving employers

**Default rank 39 of 50. Total 70 out of 100.** Legal. Regulated: legal. Written for the US, needs local rules and sources elsewhere.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Managing attorney of a small immigration firm that handles work visas for companies.

## Who the booked calls are with

HR leads and founders at companies that hire foreign nationals.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

Employer clients bring repeat filings, unlike a single family case. The attorney is at capacity on casework and has no one reaching out to HR teams.

## A ready offer

I book calls with HR leaders who hire internationally. Your firm reviews and approves every word and every name before anything is sent, and you pay a flat service fee.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], your firm's [real practice page or article] on employer visas was clear. Companies that sponsor workers tend to need the same help every year, and I can arrange introductions with their HR leads, with every message cleared by you first. Would a flat fee pilot be worth discussing?

## Where to find the leads

- Firm websites and AILA lawyer search where its terms allow
- Google Maps for business immigration attorney
- For their customers: companies that publish sponsored roles and US Department of Labor public disclosure data on visa filings

## What to avoid

- Contacting individual workers about their status
- Any pay per client or share of fees arrangement
- Messages that sound like legal advice or predict an outcome

## Compliance note

Lawyer advertising and direct contact are governed by state bar rules based on the ABA Model Rules (Rules 7.2 and 7.3 cover advertising and direct contact). Paying per booked client can raise problems around payments for recommendations and fee sharing. Use a flat fee, have the lawyer approve every message, and get the firm's ethics counsel or state bar guidance before you start. This is not legal advice.
