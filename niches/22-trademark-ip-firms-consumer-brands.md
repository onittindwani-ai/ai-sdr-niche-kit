# Trademark and small IP firms for consumer brands

**Default rank 44 of 50. Total 68 out of 100.** Legal. Regulated: legal. Written for the US, needs local rules and sources elsewhere.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Solo or two lawyer IP firm that files trademarks for small brands and founders.

## Who the booked calls are with

Founders of new consumer brands and small ecommerce companies.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

Brand owners often delay trademark work until someone copies them, and a solo lawyer has no time to reach them early. Each client can come back for later filings.

## A ready offer

I book calls with founders of growing consumer brands who have not protected their name yet. Your firm approves every message and pays a flat service fee.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], your [real post or guide] explained trademark classes simply. Founders tend to think about this only after a copycat shows up, so I would like to arrange earlier conversations with new consumer brands, with your approval on each message. Would that fit how you take on clients?

## Where to find the leads

- USPTO public attorney records and firm sites
- Google Maps for trademark attorney
- For their customers: new brand launches on ecommerce sites and startup directories

## What to avoid

- Sending anything that looks like an official trademark notice
- Telling a brand its mark is unprotected without a real search by the lawyer
- Volume filing mills

## Compliance note

Lawyer advertising and direct contact are governed by state bar rules based on the ABA Model Rules, and practising before the USPTO has its own rules. Scam notices that imitate trademark offices are a known problem, so your message must never look official. Flat fee only, lawyer approves all wording. This is not legal advice.
