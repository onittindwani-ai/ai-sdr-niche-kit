# Small CPA firms wanting business tax and advisory clients

**Default rank 25 of 50. Total 74 out of 100.** Finance and accounting. Regulated: finance. Written for the US, needs local rules and sources elsewhere.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Owner CPA of a firm with a few staff who wants fewer simple returns and more business clients.

## Who the booked calls are with

Owners of established small businesses who have outgrown their current tax preparer.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

A business client pays more over many years than a personal return, but partners are buried in deadlines for much of the year and do not market when they have time to.

## A ready offer

I book calls with business owners who need more than a once a year tax return. You approve the wording, and I work around your busy season.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], I noticed you focus on [real niche or service]. Business owners often stay with a preparer they have outgrown, because no one offers an alternative at the right time. I can book those calls for you outside of busy season. Could I show you who I would contact?

## Where to find the leads

- State board of accountancy licensee lookups
- AICPA and state society firm finders where public
- For their customers: local chambers of commerce and industry association member lists

## What to avoid

- Outreach during the weeks before a filing deadline
- Promising tax savings of any amount
- Implying the firm has reviewed the prospect's returns

## Compliance note

CPAs answer to their state board, and professionals practising before the IRS are bound by Treasury Circular 230, which covers conduct including solicitation. Never promise a tax outcome, and have the licensee approve all wording. This is not legal or tax advice.
