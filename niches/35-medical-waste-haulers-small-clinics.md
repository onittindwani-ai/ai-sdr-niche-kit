# Medical waste haulers for small clinics

**Default rank 43 of 50. Total 69 out of 100.** Health. Regulated: health. Written for the US, needs local rules and sources elsewhere.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Owner of a regional medical waste pickup company that competes with one or two national haulers.

## Who the booked calls are with

Office managers at dental offices, veterinary clinics and small medical practices.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

Pickups repeat for years and small accounts are often unhappy with contract terms at larger haulers. A regional owner wants those accounts, but each one is small.

## A ready offer

I book calls with office managers along your pickup routes who want to compare their current hauler's contract and service.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], small clinics often sign a waste contract once and forget about it until the auto renewal surprises them. I can book calls with office managers along your routes who want another option. Could I send a sample of the clinics I found?

## Where to find the leads

- State environmental agency lists of licensed medical waste transporters
- Google Maps for medical waste disposal
- For their customers: the NPI Registry and state dental and veterinary licence lists

## What to avoid

- Asserting a competitor's contract is illegal or unfair
- Giving regulatory advice about waste handling
- Using a clinic's specific waste details

## Compliance note

Medical waste is regulated at the state level and the transport is permitted. Do not give handling advice, and confirm the client's permits for each state before you promote them. This is not legal advice.
