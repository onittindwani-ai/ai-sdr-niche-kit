# Fire extinguisher and sprinkler inspection companies

**Default rank 10 of 50. Total 79 out of 100.** Commercial trades. Not in a regulated group. Written for the US, needs local rules and sources elsewhere.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Owner of a local fire protection service company with a handful of inspectors and a route based business.

## Who the booked calls are with

Facility managers, property managers, restaurant groups and small industrial sites.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

Inspections repeat on a schedule, so each new account pays back over and over. Winning one means reaching the person whose current vendor has been slipping, and the owner has no one assigned to do that.

## A ready offer

I book calls with building and facility managers near your routes who want a second quote on inspections, so each new account adds to work your team already drives past.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], saw that you cover [real service area]. Inspection accounts come back every year, so one good introduction goes a long way. I book short calls with facility managers near your routes who are open to a second quote. Would you like to see who I would approach first?

## Where to find the leads

- State fire marshal licence lists for fire protection companies
- NAFED and NFSA member search where public
- For their customers: property management firms and multi site restaurant owners in the service area

## What to avoid

- Suggesting a building is out of code, which you cannot know
- Using scare wording about fires
- Owners without the state licences their work needs

## Compliance note

Inspection work is licensed in many states. Confirm the licence for each state you promote them in, and never tell a prospect their building fails code.
