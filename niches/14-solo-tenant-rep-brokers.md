# Solo commercial tenant representation brokers

**Default rank 12 of 50. Total 79 out of 100.** Property and built environment. Not in a regulated group. The idea works in most countries, though the named lead sources are often US ones.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Independent commercial real estate broker who represents tenants and depends on personal relationships for deals.

## Who the booked calls are with

Business owners and office managers whose leases are likely to come up in the next year or two.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

One lease can be worth a lot to a solo broker, and the hard part is reaching a tenant before the landlord's agent does. Timing is everything and nobody is doing that research for them.

## A ready offer

I find businesses likely to be near a lease decision and book short calls for you, so you are advising them before they renew by default.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], I noticed you work the tenant side in [market]. Most renewals happen by default because no one calls the tenant early enough. I research companies likely to be near a lease decision and book the conversation for you. Is that a problem worth 10 minutes?

## Where to find the leads

- State real estate licence lookups
- Brokerage sites and CCIM or SIOR member search where public
- For their customers: company growth signals such as hiring pages, and business news for local moves

## What to avoid

- Paying an unlicensed person per deal, which many states forbid
- Claiming you know a company's lease date
- Brokers with a team and an in house researcher

## Compliance note

Real estate brokerage is licensed and many states restrict sharing commission with unlicensed people. Charge a flat service fee for outreach, never a share of commission, and check the state rules.
