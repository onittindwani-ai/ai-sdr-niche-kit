# Commercial flat roof repair contractors

**Default rank 3 of 50. Total 81 out of 100.** Commercial trades. Not in a regulated group. The idea works in most countries, though the named lead sources are often US ones.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Owner of a commercial roofing company with one or two crews who still walks the roofs and writes the quotes.

## Who the booked calls are with

Property managers, facility managers and owners of warehouses, strip malls and office buildings with flat roofs.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

The owner is on a roof all day, so nobody is calling property managers between storms. One building manager can bring repeat repairs across a whole portfolio, which is worth far more than a single job.

## A ready offer

I book intro calls with property managers who run flat roof buildings in your service area. You approve the list and the wording, and you pay for calls that meet the definition we agree in writing.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], I saw the [real job from their site] reroof. If your next job depends on who calls you after a leak, I can put you in front of property managers before the leak happens. I book the intro calls and you only take the ones in your area. Want to see the list I would start with?

## Where to find the leads

- Your state or province contractor licence lookup, filtered to roofing
- Google Maps searches for commercial roofing by metro area
- For their customers: local IREM and BOMA chapter member lists where public, and the manager names on office and industrial listing sites

## What to avoid

- Storm chasers who only want insurance jobs
- Residential only roofers, because homeowners are not reachable by cold email
- Repeating any warranty or leak free claim you cannot see in writing
