# Independent commercial insurance agencies for contractors

**Default rank 48 of 50. Total 64 out of 100.** Finance and accounting. Regulated: finance. Written for the US, needs local rules and sources elsewhere.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Principal of an independent insurance agency writing general liability and workers compensation for contractors.

## Who the booked calls are with

Owners and office managers at small contracting companies.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

Policies renew each year, so a new account pays back over time. Agencies wait for renewals to come to them, and principals still spend their days on service work.

## A ready offer

I book renewal review calls with contractors in your state ahead of their policy date, so you get the first look instead of the last.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], I see you write a lot of [real line, such as contractor liability]. Contractors tend to shop their policy only days before renewal, which leaves no real time to compare. I book earlier review calls for you. Is that useful for your agency?

## Where to find the leads

- State insurance department agent lookups
- Carrier agent locators and Big I association member lists
- For their customers: state contractor licence lists

## What to avoid

- Quoting premiums or savings in any message
- Advertising coverage an agent is not appointed to sell
- Claiming to know a contractor's renewal date

## Compliance note

Insurance is licensed state by state, and unlicensed people generally cannot discuss coverage or quote. Your role stops at booking the call. Do not discuss terms or prices, and do not take a share of commission unless a licensed professional confirms it is allowed.
