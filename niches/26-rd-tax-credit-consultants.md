# R and D tax credit consultants

**Default rank 49 of 50. Total 64 out of 100.** Finance and accounting. Regulated: finance. Depends on US law or US programs.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Owner of a small R and D credit study firm, often a former CPA or engineer.

## Who the booked calls are with

Owners of small manufacturers, engineering firms and software companies.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

Every client is a sizeable engagement, but the niche depends on outbound and competition is heavy. Small firms lose out to larger ones with sales teams.

## A ready offer

I book calls with owners of manufacturers and engineering firms who have not looked at this credit with their own CPA. Your firm reviews all wording before it goes out.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], since you work with [real industry], you have probably seen owners who never had this credit looked at properly. I can book calls with those owners, with your firm approving the wording. Would you like to see how I would explain it without overpromising?

## Where to find the leads

- AICPA and tax credit consultant association lists
- Google Maps for R and D tax credit consultants
- For their customers: state manufacturing association members and engineering firm lists

## What to avoid

- Any promise of a credit or a dollar figure
- Contingent fee structures you have not had reviewed
- Firms that use aggressive claims about what qualifies

## Compliance note

This area gets IRS attention. Practitioners are bound by Treasury Circular 230, and promising a credit amount can mislead. Use no dollar figures, and have the licensed professional approve every message. This is not tax advice.
