# Bookkeeping firms specialised in contractors

**Default rank 18 of 50. Total 77 out of 100.** Finance and accounting. Regulated: finance. The idea works in most countries, though the named lead sources are often US ones.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Owner of a small bookkeeping firm that focuses on construction and trade businesses.

## Who the booked calls are with

Owners and office managers at contracting companies with messy job costing.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

Monthly clients stay for a long time, and specialists can charge more than generalists. The owner is usually doing the books and finds it hard to step out to sell.

## A ready offer

I book calls with contractors whose job costing is a mess, so you get clients who need a specialist and not just someone to enter receipts.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], specialising in contractors is smart, because job costing is where general bookkeepers tend to struggle. I can reach out to trade business owners near you and book calls with the ones who know their numbers are behind. Would you like to see a sample list?

## Where to find the leads

- Directories from accounting software partner programs
- Google Maps and professional bookkeeper association member lists
- For their customers: state contractor licence lists

## What to avoid

- Claiming a bookkeeper can give tax advice
- Owners who already have more clients than they can handle
- Any claim about how much money you will find them

## Compliance note

Bookkeeping is lightly regulated in many places, but tax advice is not. Keep promises to organised books and on time reports, and do not imply tax or audit services the firm is not licensed to give.
