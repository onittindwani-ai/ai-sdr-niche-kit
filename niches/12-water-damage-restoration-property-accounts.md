# Water damage restoration firms winning property manager accounts

**Default rank 26 of 50. Total 74 out of 100.** Commercial trades. Not in a regulated group. The idea works in most countries, though the named lead sources are often US ones.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Owner of an independent restoration company that lives on emergencies and wants a preferred vendor relationship.

## Who the booked calls are with

Property managers, HOA managers, facility directors and commercial insurance adjusters.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

A preferred vendor spot with a manager means repeat emergency work without bidding each time. Owners know this and try to win those spots, but crews come first.

## A ready offer

I book intro meetings with property managers who do not yet have a restoration vendor they trust, so you are on their list before the next burst pipe.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], when a pipe bursts at 2 in the morning, a manager calls the name already on their list. I book intro calls so that name is [their company], starting with managers who run older buildings near you. Would you like the first batch of names?

## Where to find the leads

- IICRC certified firm locator
- Google Maps for water damage restoration
- For their customers: IREM and community association management directories where public

## What to avoid

- Paying or promising anything to a manager in return for referrals
- Contacting people who just had a loss
- Franchises with a national call center

## Compliance note

Some states restrict how restoration firms deal with claims and adjusters. Keep the offer to introductions, and never suggest how a claim should be handled.
