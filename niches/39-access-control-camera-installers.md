# Access control and camera system installers

**Default rank 17 of 50. Total 77 out of 100.** Technology. Not in a regulated group. The idea works in most countries, though the named lead sources are often US ones.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Owner of a small low voltage company that installs door access and camera systems.

## Who the booked calls are with

Facility managers, school administrators, property managers and multi site retailers.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

Each installed system can lead to a monthly service contract. The owner is usually pulling cable and quoting at the same time.

## A ready offer

I book site review calls with facility and property managers near you, focused on buildings with older door hardware and no service agreement.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], your [real installation] looks like clean work. Older buildings often run on systems nobody wants to touch, and that is usually where a good installer wins. I book site review calls with those facility managers. Would you like to see the first 10 buildings I would try?

## Where to find the leads

- State low voltage or security contractor licence lists
- Manufacturer dealer locators
- For their customers: school administrator pages, property management firms and office park tenant lists

## What to avoid

- Mentioning a recent crime at the property
- Claiming any system prevents break ins
- Security licence states where the installer is not registered

## Compliance note

Security and low voltage work is licensed in many states, and school projects have their own purchasing rules. Confirm licences first and do not make safety guarantees.
