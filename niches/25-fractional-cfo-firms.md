# Fractional CFO firms

**Default rank 20 of 50. Total 76 out of 100.** Finance and accounting. Regulated: finance. The idea works in most countries, though the named lead sources are often US ones.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Solo fractional CFO or a small group of them, mostly selling through their own network.

## Who the booked calls are with

Founders and owners who have outgrown their bookkeeper and need help with cash, pricing or raising capital.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

Referrals dry up without warning and most fractional CFOs have no pipeline of their own. A single retained client is meaningful, so a few well matched calls can change a year.

## A ready offer

I book calls with owners who have recently hit a growth trigger, like a funding round or a big hire. You only speak with companies that fit your industry focus.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], I saw you work mainly with [real industry]. Referrals are great until they stop, so I look for companies that just hit a trigger such as a funding round or a big hire, and book the call. Could I send you three examples from this month?

## Where to find the leads

- LinkedIn profiles reviewed by hand, never scraped or automated
- Fractional executive networks and their member pages
- For their customers: funding news, job posts for finance roles and industry association lists

## What to avoid

- Describing a company's finances from guesswork
- Anyone calling themselves a CFO with no real finance background
- Automating LinkedIn, which its terms bar

## Compliance note

If the work touches securities, investment advice or fundraising, licensing rules can apply. Keep the offer to internal finance leadership and have the client confirm what they are allowed to do.
