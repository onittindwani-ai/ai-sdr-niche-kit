# Occupational health clinics selling to employers

**Default rank 40 of 50. Total 70 out of 100.** Health. Regulated: health. Depends on US law or US programs.

Scores are judgment scores against [RUBRIC.md](../RUBRIC.md), not market data.

## Who the buyer is

Owner or operator of an independent clinic offering drug testing, DOT physicals and injury care for employers.

## Who the booked calls are with

HR managers, safety managers and owners at trucking, construction and manufacturing companies.

## Why they would pay for booked calls

Employers send workers repeatedly once they have an account. The clinic is set up to treat people, not to knock on the doors of local employers.

## A ready offer

I book calls with safety and HR managers near your clinic, so you can set up employer accounts for testing and physicals.

## Opening message

Replace every part in square brackets with something true you found yourself. Never send it with a detail you made up.

> [First name], I noticed you handle [real service, such as DOT physicals] near [area]. Local employers tend to pick a clinic once and stay, so I book short calls with their safety and HR managers for you. Could I share the list of nearby employers I would try first?

## Where to find the leads

- FMCSA National Registry of Certified Medical Examiners
- Google Maps for occupational health clinics
- For their customers: FMCSA carrier lookups, state contractor lists and local manufacturers

## What to avoid

- Referencing any worker's test result or injury
- Promising faster return to work
- Pay per patient outreach

## Compliance note

Clinics handle protected health information, so stay away from any employee health data. Medical examiners who do commercial driver exams work through the FMCSA national registry. Fee arrangements linked to patient volume can raise problems under the federal Anti Kickback Statute where federal programs pay. This is not legal advice.
