# The scoring rubric

Scores are judgment scores against this rubric. They are not market data. Every score is 1 to 5, and a higher score is always better for you. One person scored all 50 using these written rules, so you can disagree with any score and change it. This file is generated by tools/build_docs.py from data/. Change the data, then run that tool.

## How the total works

Each niche gets a score from 1 to 5 on each of the 7 criteria. Each score is multiplied by the weight, and the results are added up. The weights add up to 20, so the lowest possible total is 20 and the highest is 100.

| Criterion | Key | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner has no sales team | `no_sales_team` | 4 |
| Value of one booked call | `call_value` | 4 |
| How findable the buyers are | `findability` | 3 |
| How reachable by email | `email_reach` | 3 |
| Seasonality (steady all year scores high) | `steady_demand` | 2 |
| Compliance risk (low risk scores high) | `compliance_safety` | 2 |
| How crowded (a quiet market scores high) | `open_field` | 2 |

## What each score means

### Owner has no sales team

Does the owner personally do the selling, with nobody to set up or run sales software?

- **1:** Most firms like this already have salespeople or a sales manager.
- **3:** About half and half. The owner sells, but some firms have a rep.
- **5:** The owner does nearly all the selling, between doing the actual work.
- **2 and 4** sit between those.

### Value of one booked call

If one booked call turns into a customer, how much is that worth to this owner, compared with what they would pay you?

- **1:** One new customer is small. It would take many to justify a monthly fee.
- **3:** One new customer would plausibly cover a month of your fee.
- **5:** One new customer is large or stays for years, so a few calls can justify the whole service.
- **2 and 4** sit between those.

### How findable the buyers are

Can you list both sides from public sources: the owners who would pay you, and the customers you would book calls with? Score the weaker side.

- **1:** One side has no public list and needs guesswork.
- **3:** Both sides can be listed, with real manual research.
- **5:** Both sides appear in public licence records, permits or directories.
- **2 and 4** sit between those.

### How reachable by email

Do both sides have business email addresses they read? Score the weaker side.

- **1:** One side mostly has no public business email (for example consumers or field crews).
- **3:** Addresses exist, but they are often shared inboxes or read rarely.
- **5:** Named people read their business email as part of the job.
- **2 and 4** sit between those.

### Seasonality (steady all year scores high)

Can this owner take new calls in every month of the year?

- **1:** Most of the year's buying happens in one season.
- **3:** Clear busy and quiet periods, but work all year.
- **5:** Steady need in every month.
- **2 and 4** sit between those.

### Compliance risk (low risk scores high)

How much could rules restrict the messages you send, or how you get paid?

- **1:** Strict professional or referral rules. The usual pay per call deal may not be allowed.
- **3:** Licensing or marketing rules that need written sign off from the client.
- **5:** Only the ordinary rules for commercial email apply.
- **2 and 4** sit between those.

### How crowded (a quiet market scores high)

How rarely do these owners hear this pitch from other outreach services?

- **1:** Owners get pitched about leads constantly.
- **3:** Some outreach services work this niche, but it is not saturated.
- **5:** Almost nobody is doing this outreach.
- **2 and 4** sit between those.

## How your own fit score works

`tools/rank.py` starts with the base score, using your weights, and scales it to 100. Then it adjusts for you:

- **Experience:** one matching experience tag adds 10 points. Two or more add 15. This is on purpose: knowing a niche well is the whole idea of this kit.
- **Country:** use the country your customers are in. If that is outside the US, a niche marked US first loses 10 points and a niche marked US only is left out, with the reason printed.
- **Your own overrides:** you can change any single score for any niche in a profile file, and you must write the reason.

Because experience is added on top of a score out of 100, your fit score can go above 100. That is expected.
